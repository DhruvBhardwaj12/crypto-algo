# LAB-018 — Deribit Volatility Risk Premium (VRP)

## Finding
BTC DVOL averaged +4.73% above subsequent 30-day realized vol
(t = +10.73, n = 940 days). Statistically significant.
ETH showed no premium (mean -0.13%, t = -0.22).

## Strategy tested
Short variance when VRP > +3%, long when VRP < -3%, 30-day non-overlapping.

## Results
BTC: Sharpe +0.01 (FULL), -0.20 (2025-2026). No edge.
ETH: Sharpe -1.14 (FULL), -0.80 (2025-2026). Negative.

## Why it failed
The threshold-based entry fires most often when trailing vol is
unusually low and DVOL stays elevated. Those are exactly the days
before a vol spike — the worst days to be short vol.

The unconditional premium (+4.73%) is real. The conditional subset
selected by the threshold is not profitable.

## Correct construction (not tested, not feasible at our scale)
Constant 5%-notional short position every day, 30-day hold, 30 positions
overlapping. Expected +7%/year gross. Requires tail hedge (buying cheap
OTM puts) to survive vol spikes. Hedge costs consume most of the premium
at retail capital.

## Verdict: REJECTED for our scale.

The underlying edge is real but structurally inaccessible to a retail
account of ₹10-30k. Would require derivatives infrastructure, tail
hedging, and $100k+ capital to be profitable.

## Key learning
A real statistical premium does not automatically become a tradeable
strategy. The condition that selects entries can be anti-correlated
with the premium's capture. Always test the strategy, not just the
raw signal.
